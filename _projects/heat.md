## About Me


![Profile Picture]({{ "assets/images/profile-pic.jpg" | relative_url }}){: class="profile-image"}

In my opinion, the most educational homework was the second one this semester
which demonstrated, through principles of heat transfer, the cost savings of insulation
on a hot water heater, what changes could be made to improve heat flux through a
surface, and how to read a heat profile and relate that to heat flux vectors, which is a
core part of this course. Often, the principles of heat flux bridges together where we
want that heat (in this case, to stay inside the heater), and how could we accomplish it,
whether it be through reducing the length of the material, increasing surface area, or
adding insulation. This is particularly important in mechanical engineering as heat is an
inevitable byproduct of moving parts and it’s essential that certain temperatures never
be exceeded to avoid structural failure (e.g. through material deformation or
degradation). Also, pinning the amount of heat loss to a numerical value helps us
calculate performance metrics like efficiency and cost savings, which have significant
implications in the real world, as well as possible solutions to mitigate it.
